# Checking the 2.4 estimates

Eras build plan step 2.4 fills what Jolpica and F1DB can't supply: tyre compounds, safety-car
laps and (1994-2009) fuel loads. All of them are estimates, and the race file says so in
`provenance.estimated`. Only the safety-car laps need your check, race by race. The other two
are listed here so you know what they are.

## Where they come from

`pipeline jolpica <race-id>` (before 2011 it takes the stops from `data/raw/f1db/`, so run
`pipeline f1db <race-id>` first) writes:

- `data/raw/<race-id>.parquet` and `.status.json`, with the estimated Compound and TrackStatus
  filled in;
- `data/raw/<race-id>.jolpica.json`, whose `estimates` block lists every estimate: each stint's
  compound and why, each SC period, each stint's fuel load, and any data quirks;
- `data/estimates/<race-id>.sc.json`, the safety-car hand-check file.

`pipeline build <race-id>` prints the same estimates in the build report and sets
`provenance.estimated` in the race file.

## Safety cars: the hand-check

The pipeline finds the laps where the whole field ran slowly. Each period in
`data/estimates/<race-id>.sc.json` looks like this:

```json
{"startLap": 9, "endLap": 14, "laps": 6, "kind": "SC", "confidence": "likely",
 "peakRatio": 1.472, "gapBefore": 12.3, "gapAfter": 2.1, "reasons": [], "status": "unchecked"}
```

- `startLap` / `endLap` are race laps (the leader's). They include the lap the SC came out on
  and the lap it went in, as FastF1 marks them.
- `peakRatio` is how much slower than green the slowest lap was. A safety car is usually 1.3-1.7x.
- `gapBefore` / `gapAfter` are the gap from the leader to P6 before the period and at its
  tightest. A safety car bunches the field up; rain doesn't.
- `confidence` is `likely` (slow enough and the field bunched) or `ambiguous`, with the
  `reasons`: rain, yellow flags, a short SC, a red flag, or a single slow lap.

Until you check them, a `likely` period is applied as a safety car (TrackStatus `4`, a frozen SC
event in the sim). An `ambiguous` one is applied as a yellow (`2`): it's kept out of the pace fit,
but there's no SC event.

### How to check a race

1. Open the race's report (Wikipedia's race article, or the FIA's race-control documents) and
   find each safety car: the lap it came out and the lap it went in.
2. For each period in the file:
   - It matches a real SC: set `"status": "confirmed"`. Fix `startLap` / `endLap` if they're off
     by more than a lap (count the lap it came out on and the lap it went in).
   - It was rain, yellow flags or nothing: set `"status": "rejected"`.
   - It was a red flag: set `"status": "confirmed"` and `"kind": "RED"`.
3. A real SC the file doesn't list: add a period with `startLap`, `endLap`, `"kind": "SC"` and
   `"status": "confirmed"` (the other fields can be left out).
4. Re-run `pipeline jolpica <race-id> --force`, then `pipeline build <race-id>`.

Re-running keeps every `confirmed` and `rejected` period exactly as you wrote it. A fresh
detection that overlaps one of them is dropped. Only `unchecked` periods are re-detected.
Before 2015 there's no VSC, so every neutralisation is an SC or a red flag.

## Compounds (not hand-checked)

- **Before 2007:** one dry compound per race, labelled MEDIUM.
- **From 2007:** two compounds, SOFT (option) and HARD (prime), one per stint. The labels say
  softer and harder, not the real names that weekend. Each stint is scored on two things:
  - its tyre life: a softer tyre wears faster and is stopped sooner;
  - its pace against the field on the same laps.

  The softer-scoring half are SOFT. A car that stopped must use both (the rule since 2007), so if
  it didn't, the stint closest to the line flips. On the 2018-2025 FastF1 races that used two dry
  compounds, this gets 79% of stints right. In the refuelling years (2007-09) stint length also
  reflects the fuel plan, so expect it to do worse there. That hasn't been measured: there are no
  real compounds for those years.
- Wet tyres aren't estimated (a wet race needs its weather block, step 4.5).
- 2005: tyres weren't changed at stops, but TyreLife still restarts at each stop until
  `rules.tyreChanges` (step 4.1).
- Races with real compounds (TUM, FastF1, OpenF1) are never relabelled.

## Fuel loads (1994-2009, not hand-checked)

Each stint's fuel at its start is its laps times the era's standard burn
(`headset.json` `fuel.burnPerLap`, 1.7 kg a lap). For a car that retired, it's the laps the car
ran. The real burn was higher and teams carried a margin, so treat these as shapes, not weights.
The race file has no field for them yet (step 4.2), so they're in the sidecar and the build
report. Until step 4.2 a refuelling race is also built with no race-long fuel effect
(`fuelEffectPerLap` 0). The lap-number slope the fit uses means nothing when the car is
refilled at every stop.

## Data quirks the build report flags

- **An official time rounded to 0.1 s** (Hungary 1998: Schumacher's 1:45:26.4 in Jolpica). Every
  lead-lap finisher's summed laps are then off by the same amount (-0.849 s there). The gaps
  between cars are still right.
- **A driver whose summed laps disagree with the official time** by more than the field's common
  offset. This is usually a post-race time penalty: Europe 2012 MAL +20 s, Saudi Arabia 2021
  VER +5 s, Monaco 2022 OCO +5 s. It can also be a gap in the timing (Brazil 2009 KOV: the
  official time is 25 s shorter than his laps). The build uses the laps order. Check the
  official classification against the race file's `realResult`.
