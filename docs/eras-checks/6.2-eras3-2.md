# Eras 6.2: authoring Era 3 (1994-95) and Era 2 (1990-93)

Ten races: Era 3 spain-1994, australia-1994, belgium-1995; Era 2 united-states-1990,
mexico-1990, brazil-1991, belgium-1992, hungary-1992, europe-1993, australia-1993. All ten are
**reconstructed** (no lap timing survives). Every briefing ends with a provenance line saying the
laps are rebuilt and the lap-by-lap gaps and order are synthetic. No briefing, storyline or
headline states a synthetic detail as fact. Headlines are original wording, and no team radio
is quoted.

Files: `data/eras/<id>.json` (the `eras` block, plus `storylineEvents` for belgium-1995),
`data/moments/<id>.json` (no file for mexico-1990), and the registry in
`pipeline/src/pipeline/build.py` (side and rival changes, listed below).

Check each fact against its source, then put your initials and the date in **Owner checked**.

### Rival changes (owner please confirm)

Eras 2-3 race the rival with the **Expert** policy, which takes the slicks or wets on the
crossover and runs its own grader. When the storyline rival is the car whose late call decided
the real race, the Expert rival fixes that call and the storyline can't happen:

- **belgium-1992**: with Mansell as the Expert rival, Mansell wins every checked path by 10-20 s
  (Mansell's real slick stop was lap 33, Schumacher's lap 30: Facts, MS). The rival is now
  **Patrese** (the other Williams, P3), and Mansell runs his real strategy.
- **europe-1993**: with Hill as the Expert rival, Hill wins every checked path by more than a
  minute. The rival is now **Prost** (P3, seven pit calls to Senna's four: WP), and Hill runs his
  real strategy.

### Blocked (owner decision needed)

1. **australia-1994: frozen storyline retirement.** Both cars retire on their real laps (35, 36)
   whatever the calls, so ★1 (beat Hill) can't be met. Another agent is building avoidable
   storyline retirements and will author the condition. The target and objectives here don't
   depend on how it's done; once the collision is avoidable, re-run the checker.
2. **mexico-1990: the reconstruction doesn't hold the storyline.** The synthetic laps put Prost
   in the lead by lap 31 (the takeover) and Senna about 30 s back, and Prost wins every grader
   playout (every candidate call is 100% on every option). WP has Senna leading until Prost
   passes him on lap 60. The mid-race order isn't anchored. No moment has any tension, so none
   was authored. A fix is in the reconstruction (an order anchor for laps 1-60) or a later
   takeover, not in the content.
3. **hungary-1992: the grader rates "stay out" above the real stop.** After an option, the
   grader hands the car to the "sensible strategist" continuation, which plans the best later
   stop. So "Stay out" at lap 61 grades 80% (green) and "Box" 60% (red). In live play, with no
   plan, staying out never stops and finishes P4 on seeds 1-3. Boxing on lap 61 finishes P2 on
   all three, but that option is cut as red. Moments at laps 64 and 66 grade the same way, so the
   original set (stop-61, pace-66, pace-70) is kept. This is a checker and grader design gap: the
   checker doesn't play a takeover plan.

### Systemic findings (sim, not changed here)

- **Grader playouts don't stop a dry car** (sim.ts forkRun: `tankLive` is cleared for
  playouts). In a refuelling race, "Stay out" with a near-empty tank grades green, but in live
  play the car runs dry. In belgium-1995 the real stops carry the real short fills (5-6 laps),
  so every path the checker kept ran dry by lap 28. Its moments now carry `fill: "standard"` on
  every pit call. That is a fill the player can pick, and fuel loads are already estimated
  (provenance). spain-1994 and australia-1994 keep the real fills.
- **The Expert rival fixes the storyline rival's real mistake** (see "Rival changes"). Expect
  this in other Era 1-3 races whose storyline turns on the rival's late call.
