# 4.5 hand check: wet races before 2018

Eras build plan step 4.5 (roadmap R3). Before 2018 there is no weather data, so
`pipeline wet-laps <race-id>` (`pipeline/src/pipeline/wet_laps.py`) fits when the track was
wet from the lap times alone, and `pipeline build` turns that into the race's wetness
timeline with today's wet model (`wet.py`, curves in `data/wet.json`). This file sets each
fitted timeline beside what published race reports say. The reports are quoted, not
paraphrased from memory. Laps the fit can't call are flagged, not guessed.

How to read the columns:
- **Fitted wet laps:** laps where the field ran at least 3% slower than its own dry pace
  (green laps), plus the safety-car laps inside a wet spell.
- **Fitted SC laps:** from the source's status timeline (TUM) or, with none (Jolpica), from
  the lap times: the field 25%+ slower and bunched up.
- **Fitted wetness:** the built race's `weather.wetness` (0 dry .. 1 standing water).
- **Owner checked:** left blank for the owner to sign off.

## hungary-2014

**Sources:**
- Wikipedia, [2014 Hungarian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Hungarian_Grand_Prix)
  (read Nov 7 2026).

**Reported:**
- "Thunderstorms and heavy rain soaked the track at around lunchtime, forcing the drivers to
  start on intermediates ... before the track dried out later on in the race."
- Weather box: "Overcast. Wet track at start, then drying."
- "All the runners began the race on intermediate tyres."
- Lap 9: "a crash by Marcus Ericsson meant that the safety car was deployed." "All the drivers
  pitted under the safety car ... all switching to dry tyres except the two McLarens who put
  on more intermediates."
- "racing resumed on lap 14."
- Lap 16: "Button pitted from the lead for dry tyres on lap 16 ... as it became clear that the
  track was drying."
- "On lap 23 Pérez spun at the final corner and crashed into the pit wall ... This brought out
  another safety car." "The safety car came in on lap 27."
- None of the quoted report text mentions rain during the race.

**Fitted** (`pipeline wet-laps hungary-2014`, TUM snapshot):
- Wet laps 1-16. SC laps 8-13 and 23-26 (TUM status; lap 8 is the lap the first SC came out).
- Field slowing against dry pace: about 16-20% on laps 2-8 (inters), 10% on lap 14, 8% on lap 15,
  4.7% on lap 16, 3.4% on lap 17, then dry.
- Ambiguous: lap 17 (borderline slowing, 2-3% over dry: the last of the drying, or tyre
  wear); laps 7, 14, 22 and 27 (edges of an SC); laps 68-70 (borderline slowing at the end of
  the race, with no rain reported: tyre wear and the fight for the win).
- Built wetness: 0.37 at the start, falling steadily under the SC to 0.23 by the lap-14
  restart, 0.11 on lap 17, dry from lap 18. A 0.06 blip on lap 27 (the restart lap after the
  second SC). 0.03-0.08 on laps 68-70 (the borderline laps above: the dry model misses the
  closing laps).
- Tyre check: TUM's compounds agree with the report. Everyone starts on inters, BUT and MAG
  are on inters until lap 15, and everyone else is on slicks from the lap-8/9 stops. With
  TUM's compounds and status hidden, the lap-time estimate gets 98.9% of laps' tyre class
  right. It misses BUT's second set of inters. It recovers the SC laps as 9-13 and 23-26.

**Verdict:** agrees. The wet start, drying under the first SC and dry from about lap 17 all
match the report.

## brazil-2012

**Sources:**
- Wikipedia, [2012 Brazilian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Brazilian_Grand_Prix)
  (read Nov 7 2026).
- GrandPrix.com, [Brazilian GP 2012 race report](https://www.grandprix.com/races/brazilian-gp-2012-race-report-vettels-three-peat.html).
- FIA, [Button wins, Vettel World Champion](https://www.fia.com/news/button-wins-vettel-world-champion).

**Reported:**
- Wikipedia: "With only light rain just 10 minutes before the start, all teams opted to start
  on slick tyres." "Rain began to fall during the next few laps and the first cars made pit
  stops for intermediate tyres ... while Button and Hülkenberg stayed out." "the rain
  decreased shortly afterwards and cars on intermediates were forced to pit for slick tyres
  once again." "debris on the track brought out the safety car on lap 23." "At the restart on
  lap 29 ..." "When the rain started again, Vettel was one of the first to pit for
  intermediates." Weather box: "Light rain shower getting heavier at the end."
- GrandPrix.com: "Literally minutes before the start the drizzle began. It wasn't heavy
  enough to demand intermediate Pirelli tyres." "As the rain started to increase, Jenson ...
  passed Hamilton to lead the race on lap 8." "Kobayashi, Schumacher, Rosberg, Ricciardo and
  Webber had all pitted for intermediates by lap nine." "The gap was growing all the time as
  the track dried and everyone on intermediates started to struggle." "Around lap 48 though,
  heavier drizzle returned." "There followed a universal rush for intermediates as the rain
  increased." "On lap 69, with just two to go, Paul Di Resta lost the other Force India ...
  bringing about a Safety Car."
- FIA: "The start took place in light drizzle: not enough to call for intermediate tyres."
  Later, "conditions that were becoming slippery again as more rain fell", "the field going
  back onto Inters for the final stint as the rain became heavier". "The race finished under
  the safety car."

**Fitted** (`pipeline wet-laps brazil-2012`, Jolpica snapshot; SC laps and tyre classes
estimated from the lap times):
- Wet laps 6-19, 22-30 and 53-71. SC laps 25-29 and 70-71.
- Field slowing: 1-2% on laps 2-4, 3.6% on lap 5, rising to 11-13% on laps 9-15, down to 7%
  on lap 19 and 2.8% on lap 20, dry on 21. On laps 22-23, 17% and 26% with a wide spread
  across the field. 0-1% on laps 33-41, creeping up to 2-4.3% on laps 43-52, 9% on lap 53,
  11-18% from lap 54 to lap 68, 24-35% on laps 69-70 (heavier rain, di Resta's crash).
- Ambiguous: lap 5 (borderline: the rain starting); lap 23 (as slow as a safety car without
  the bunching); lap 24 (the reported SC lap: it probably came out during it); lap 30
  (restart); lap 31 and laps 50-52 (borderline: drizzle or tyre wear); lap 69 (di Resta's
  crash lap).
- Tyre classes fitted: inters for the second stint of most of the field, while BUT and HUL
  stay on slicks (as reported). Inters again for everyone's last stint from about lap 53,
  including BUT's from his lap-58 stop.
- Built wetness: 0 on laps 1-3, 0.11 by lap 5, 0.2-0.31 on laps 7-16, drying to 0.12 by lap
  19 and 0.01 on lap 21. Then 0.33-0.42 on laps 22-23, 0.19-0.24 under the SC, dry from lap
  33. 0.02-0.14 on laps 43-52, 0.24 on lap 53 and 0.28-0.34 to lap 68, 0.44 on the last
  three laps.

**Verdict:** mostly agrees. Rain comes in during the first laps, inters go on by lap 9 and the
track dries back to slicks by laps 18-20. The track is dry after the SC, drizzle returns
around lap 48 (fitted damp from lap 43, wet from 53), there's the rush for inters, and the
race ends under the SC. All of these match the reports.

**Not confirmed:** the fitted wet spike on laps 22-23 (wetness 0.33-0.42), just before the
lap-23 SC. The reports quoted above don't mention rain there. The field was 17-26% slow with
a wide spread on those laps, before it bunched up behind the SC. That fits a shower, but also
yellow flags for the debris. Owner to check (e.g. against a lap-by-lap commentary).

## Known limits

- The SC laps from the lap times start a lap or two late when the field doesn't close up at
  once. Brazil's lap-23 SC is detected from lap 25, with laps 23-24 flagged.
- Wet vs inter from lap times: the classes come from the fitted curves. A stint where both
  tyres fit equally well is decided by which tyre was faster over the stint, and keeping the
  same wet tyre at a stop is discouraged. That is why Button's second set of inters at
  Hungary 2014 is missed.
- Dry stints from a source without compounds are `MEDIUM` placeholders until step 2.4
  estimates the dry compounds.
- The dry reference is each driver's own 10th-percentile lap. A race that is wet throughout
  has no dry laps to measure against, so its wetness is relative to its driest laps.
